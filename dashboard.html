Sizing the GMV at risk

Myntra doesn’t publish GMV, so this is a chained Fermi estimate from public data — directional, not verified. Full sourcing is in the linked report.

Step 1 ₹22,000 Cr Estimated annual GMV, from FY25 revenue (₹6,043 Cr) at a ~27% marketplace take rate
→
Step 2 ~122M orders GMV ÷ ~₹1,800 average order value
→
Step 3 2.0–2.6% Est. share of orders lost to delay-driven cancellation/RTO (15–20% delayed × 13pp delay penalty)
→
Result ₹450–650 Cr / yr GMV at risk — excludes churn/LTV loss and brand damage, which likely push this higher
Key assumption to validate internally: ~15–20% of shipments miss their delivery window meaningfully. Delay→RTO penalty (22% → 35% RTO) sourced from 2026 Indian fashion e-commerce benchmarks (GoKwik/First Resort). GMV take-rate and AOV are industry-typical assumptions, not Myntra-confirmed figures.

Recommended problem to build around

When a Myntra order is at risk of missing its delivery promise, the customer gets no visibility, no proactive resolution, and — if they escalate — a support flow that can’t actually fix delivery, refund-routing, or exchange-tag mismatches.

Highest frequency (50% of complaints), most current (last 24–48h), and best-benchmarked against industry RTO data — so the GMV case is defensible, not hand-wavy.

Proactive delay + expectation resetFlag hub-dwell risk before the customer has to ask; pair with an automatic goodwill action instead of silence.
Support triage, not a chatbot loopRoute “where’s my order / refund / exchange mismatch” straight to a resolution or a human.
Refund-routing safeguardHold and flag refunds where bank details look stale or mismatched before disbursing.
